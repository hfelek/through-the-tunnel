# T8.4 Particle filter design note (minimal)

## Idea
The EKF+ZUPT gives speed, handles GNSS, stops and beacons. The particle filter adds one thing: map matching inside tunnels. It answers "where along the road am I?" by checking which positions agree with what the gyroscope and the barometer measure. It wraps an ekf_zupt instance and never replaces its GNSS logic.

## Particles
N = 500 particles, each (s_i, dv_i): position and a speed offset from the EKF speed (the EKF speed error is not known, so each particle tries a slightly different one).
While GNSS is current (EKF mode "gnss"): no map matching; the output is the EKF's; particles are re-drawn from N(s_ekf, sigma_s^2) and N(0, sigma_v^2) on every accepted fix.
Inside the tunnel: particles are propagated at 10 Hz: s_i <- s_i + (v_ekf + dv_i) * dt; dv_i follows a random walk with dv_rw (default 0.05 m/s/sqrt(s)).

## Weights (once per second, on 1 s averages)
1. Curvature: the measured yaw rate (aligner, about the vertical) should equal v * curvature(s_i). Likelihood N(yaw_meas - (v_ekf + dv_i) * curvature(s_i); sigma_yaw), sigma_yaw default 0.005 rad/s (gyro noise after averaging plus residual bias). Only when the aligner is valid.
2. Barometer: the altitude change since a reference should equal the map altitude change. Reference taken baro_ref_delay_s (default 5 s, A21) after tunnel entry: store h_ref (barometric altitude from the ISA formula) and each particle's s_ref_i. Then likelihood N((h_meas - h_ref) - (z(s_i) - z(s_ref_i)); sigma_h), sigma_h default 1.0 m (fan noise about 0.7 m plus drift). Particles keep s_ref_i through resampling.
Multiply the weights, normalize. On a straight, flat road all particles get the same weight and the result equals the EKF's: map matching cannot make it worse.

## Resampling
Systematic resampling when N_eff = 1 / sum(w^2) < N / 2, then a small jitter (1 m on s, 0.05 m/s on dv) so particles do not collapse onto copies.

## Output
s = weighted mean of s_i, sigma_s = weighted standard deviation, v = v_ekf + weighted mean of dv_i, mode from the EKF, display_s with the T8.6 rule applied to the particle s. Callouts use the particle s and sigma_s.

## Parameters (A34)
N 500, dv_rw 0.05 m/s/sqrt(s), sigma_yaw 0.005 rad/s, sigma_h 1.0 m, baro_ref_delay_s 5, jitter 1 m and 0.05 m/s. Not sourced; reasoning above.

## Expected behaviour
Error drops after a curve (curved_5km_exit_inside) or the dip (undersea_3km_dip); equal to the EKF on straight, flat tunnels (beacons_5km with beacons off).

## Addendum CR1: particle spread, barometer weighting, use_baro
1. Speed spread: at each 0.1 s step every `dv_i` also gets `N(0, sqrt(max(0, P_vv(t) - P_vv(t-dt))))`, on top of `dv_rw`, so the particles carry the EKF's speed uncertainty, which grows with the acceleration-offset uncertainty. After a redraw the reference P_vv restarts.
2. Barometer weighting only every `baro_weight_interval_s` (10 s, the A22 fan correlation time), still on a 1 s average with `sigma_h` 1 m. Consecutive 1 s samples share the same fan error, so weighting every second counted one error about ten times. Curvature weighting stays once per second.
3. `use_baro` (bool, default true). False means curvature only. The barometer helps only when the altitude change is large against the fan noise: the 48 m dip in undersea_3km_dip is about 580 Pa against about 8 Pa of fan noise; a 1% grade gives only about 12 Pa per 100 m.

## Addendum CR2: Barometer removed
The particle filter uses curvature only. The barometer's benefit in tunnels is uncertain: ventilation and vehicle-induced pressure changes are unknown and may be far above the assumed 8 Pa of fan noise (A22), and single runs showed no clear gain. Removed: the barometer likelihood, the reference altitude handling, and the parameters `use_baro`, `sigma_h`, `baro_weight_interval_s` and `baro_ref_delay_s`. The text above about the barometer (weights item 2, `sigma_h`, `baro_ref_delay_s`, the CR1 points 2 and 3) is history. With curvature only, a straight dip gives nothing to match on, so `undersea_3km_dip` is no longer a target. The barometer stays as a sensor model, trace data and chart (it shows the tunnel pressure step); no estimator uses it.

## Addendum CR3: Map matching only when the map is informative
On a straight road every particle predicts the same yaw rate, so the curvature likelihood only weights the noise in the measured yaw rate, and resampling then lets the cloud's mean wander (`speed_changes_3km`: pf 42.4 m vs ekf_zupt 17.1 m in-tunnel RMSE).
Before each 1 s weighting the filter computes the weighted std across particles of their expected yaw rates `(v_ekf + dv_i) * curvature(s_i)`. Below `informative_ratio * sigma_yaw` (0.5) weighting and resampling are skipped for that second.
Recentring the cloud on the EKF after `no_info_s` of no weighting was tried and removed (CR4): the EKF does not know the correction the particles made in a curve, so recentring threw it away (`curved_5km_exit_inside` pf RMSE 16.6 m before CR3, 39.1 m with recentring, 21.1 m without). Skipping alone does not fix `speed_changes_3km` (pf 42.4 m vs ekf_zupt 17.1 m): there the weights were already equal and no resampling happened, so the skip changes nothing; the gap comes from elsewhere.

## Addendum CR5: Particle propagation check
All random draws in `_propagate` and `_redraw` are per particle (size N), none is shared, so the cloud does not move together by construction. On `speed_changes_3km` the pf mean minus the EKF's `s` is 0.0 m at tunnel entry, -31.0 m after the 90 km/h point, -30.6 m after the 30 km/h point, -28.1 m after the 60 km/h point and -25.9 m at the exit: an offset that appears within about 30 s of entry and then stays, not a steady drift. Cause found in CR6: the particles were propagated only in the tunnel, i.e. after the EKF's `gnss_timeout_s` (1.5 s) had passed since the last fix, so the cloud, redrawn at that fix, stood still for 1.64 s and started 33 m behind (pf - EKF = -33.1 m at the first propagation step). Weighting cannot repair that on a straight road.

## Addendum CR6: Propagate continuously
`_propagate` now runs at every 0.1 s step once the filter has started, also while GNSS is current; particles are still redrawn around the EKF at every accepted fix, and weighting stays tunnel-only (the 1 s averages start at the first tunnel step). The offset is gone: `speed_changes_3km` pf 16.9 m vs ekf_zupt 17.1 m in-tunnel RMSE; `curved_5km_exit_inside` pf 9.8 m (was 16.6 m, ekf_zupt 45.0 m); `traffic_before_tunnel` pf 16.7 m vs 16.7 m.
