# Assumptions

Numbered assumptions. Code that depends on one references it with a comment (`# A7`). New assumptions get the next number; never renumber.

- **A1** Tunnel is single-lane, no overtaking; the vehicle's route is known from navigation.
- **A2** Map centerline, curvature and grade are accurate (error < 2 m).
- **A3** Phone is fixed in a mount; taking it out of the mount is modeled as a separate scenario.
- **A4** There are at least 60 s of good GNSS before the tunnel (calibration window).
- **A5** Sensors: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, barometer, GNSS. No vehicle speed data (OBD, CarPlay); discussed as "what if" in the write-up.
- **A6** No GNSS, cellular or Wi-Fi positioning inside the tunnel.
- **A7** Beacons exist only in scenarios that explicitly enable them.
- **A8** In simulation, sensor timestamps are synchronized and delay-free.
- **A9** The callout point is where the exit on the route begins; the driver does not miss the exit.
- **A10** With several vehicles, the single lane means a follower cannot get closer than a minimum gap to the vehicle ahead. Matters only for multi-vehicle scenarios.
- **A11** Longitudinal motion is jerk-limited at 2.0 m/s³. Speed changes the scenario gives no acceleration for (cruise to a new speed, `stop_and_go`) use 1.5 m/s². Both are typical passenger-comfort values (not sourced).
- **A12** Pitch is blended over 40 m (`VERTICAL_BLEND_M`) at grade changes, like road vertical curves, so the vertical acceleration `v² · d(pitch)/ds` has no spikes at grade steps.
- **A13** Follower braking up to 3.0 m/s², below the AASHTO comfortable deceleration of 3.4 m/s². Matters only for multi-vehicle scenarios.
- **A14** The phone's temperature changes by about 2 °C (2 K) during a 3-minute tunnel. Bias drift values in presets are derived from datasheet temperature coefficients with this assumption.
- **A15** GNSS position error is time-correlated (Gauss–Markov, 30 s); GNSS speed noise 0.15 m/s and magnetometer noise 0.3 µT in open sky are typical smartphone values, not datasheet figures.
- **A16** The budget preset is the flagship preset scaled by fixed multipliers (2–4×), since low-cost phone sensor datasheets are rarely public.
- **A17** Vibration and handling. Engine idle: 0.03 m/s² accel and 0.002 rad/s gyro RMS. Moving: accel RMS 0.03 + 0.01·v m/s², gyro RMS 0.002 + 0.0005·v rad/s (v in m/s). Tunable; chosen so that stopped and moving are separable for ZUPT. Phone handling is an orientation path, not noise: a 1 s pick-up (or T/4 for events shorter than 4 s) to a hand orientation about 15° per axis from the mount; while held, a re-orientation every 10–30 s by about 20° per axis (only when the hold exceeds 10 s); hand wobble of about 5° per axis (three sinusoids per axis at 0.5–2 Hz, faded in and out so the path starts at the old mount and ends at the new one); a 1 s put-back to the new mount. The gyro sees this path exactly (its relative rotation rate is added to the vehicle's), and the accelerometer is rotated with it. On top, 2 m/s² white translational acceleration noise on the accelerometer for the event duration (not sourced). Real hand displacement is bounded, white acceleration noise is not, so estimators must not integrate acceleration while the phone is in hand. An event may run past the end of the run (no put-back). Effect: only the handling scenarios. T9.3: the stationary noise is no longer assumed anywhere in the estimator: the ZUPT bias update measures it in each 1 s window (A32). A louder idle (diesel, bus) slows the bias learning but does not bias it; what is not validated is whether the stationary detector still fires with a loud idle: with the idle vibration 5x larger it caught 0 of 39 stops on `stop_and_go_3km` (seeds 0-5), and `ekf_zupt` then behaves like `ekf`.
- **A18** GNSS vertical error is 1.5 × horizontal: satellites are only above the horizon, so vertical geometry is weaker; FAA GPS SPS Performance Analysis reports show 95% vertical/horizontal ratios of about 1.35–1.6.
- **A19** GNSS error and reported h_acc grow linearly to 5× within portal_degrade_m of a portal, on both sides, from sky blockage and multipath; tunable.
- **A20** Barometer base altitude is sea level (0 m); Istanbul's undersea tunnels start near sea level. Effect: every 100 m of base altitude changes the pressure-altitude gradient by about 1%; estimators use relative pressure only, so results are insensitive.
- **A21** The tunnel pressure step settles exponentially with a 1 s time constant at entry and reverses the same way at exit (cabin leakage and the barometer's internal IIR low-pass filter; the exact value is not sourced). Effect: only the first few seconds after each portal. No estimator uses the barometer (the PF dropped barometer matching in CR2, A34), so this only shapes the simulated signal.
- **A22** Fan-driven pressure fluctuation is first-order Gauss-Markov with a 10 s correlation time (not sourced). Effect: a longer correlation time makes fluctuations look like altitude changes and hurts barometer map matching. No estimator uses the barometer (A34, CR2), so this only shapes the simulated signal.
- **A23** Inside a tunnel the magnetic disturbance from steel reinforcement and passing vehicles is modeled as a spatial Gauss-Markov field with a 10 m correlation length and mag_disturbance_ut std per axis, generated per vehicle (not sourced); a shared per-tunnel field would be more realistic, this is a deliberate simplification. Effect: none on estimators, which do not use the magnetometer; it only supports the demonstration of why the magnetometer is excluded.
- **A24** BLE RSSI noise is independent Gaussian per packet with 4 dB std (published measurements range from a few dB to over 10 dB depending on environment; not sourced for tunnels). Effect: larger noise widens the RSSI peak and delays or shifts beacon detection in T8.5.
- **A25** The phone receives every advertisement at or above -90 dBm and none below (continuous scanning, as in Android's low-latency scan mode; iOS ranging reports about once per second instead). Effect: sets the detection range, about 25 m with the default scenario values.
- **A26** Beacons are wall-mounted 2.5 m laterally from the lane center and 2 m above the phone, so the closest approach is about 3.2 m. Effect: caps the peak RSSI and flattens the peak slightly.
- **A27** Driver speed variation while cruising is a sum of 4 cosines with random phases and frequencies drawn uniformly from 0.02-0.1 Hz (10-50 s periods), scaled so its std is speed_jitter_kmh; it fades in and out over 3 s around stops and speed changes (not sourced; this is not an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process). Effect: it dominates the baseline's dead-reckoning error in scenarios without stops (roughly 0.5 m/s times the outage duration).
- **A28** The longitudinal acceleration noise the aligner reports before any yaw uncertainty is `sigma_base` = 0.3 m/s² (not sourced). It matches the A17 vibration level at highway speed (0.03 + 0.01·v, about 0.23 m/s² per axis at 20 m/s) plus a margin; measured on the 13 scenarios the error of the extracted longitudinal acceleration is 0.14–0.26 m/s² RMS. Effect: sets how much the EKF trusts the accelerometer; the curve-dependent yaw term is added on top. T9.3: `sigma_base` is the driving-vibration level; the ZUPT bias update no longer uses it (its stationary noise is about 8x smaller, so the gain was about 2%) but the noise measured in the window (A32).
- **A29** Handling detection thresholds, from the gyro only: start when the smoothed (0.2 s moving average) angular rate, minus bias and the expected vehicle rotation, exceeds 0.15 rad/s for more than 0.2 s; end when it stays below 0.03 rad/s for 1.0 s. Reasoning: a hand pick-up or put-back of about 15° in 1 s peaks at about 0.4 rad/s and the hand wobble at 0.5-2 Hz is of similar size, while the vehicle's own rotation is about 0.02 rad/s (v/R at 20 m/s and 800 m) and gyro vibration is about 0.01 rad/s per axis (A17); the moving average is needed because raw vibration noise (3-axis magnitude about 0.02 rad/s) is only about 1.3 sigma below the end threshold. A slow, deliberate hand movement below 0.15 rad/s would go undetected. Tunable; the values were tuned on simulated data only. Effect: detection delay of about 0.1 s at the start and 1 s at the end of an event; the 1.0 s calm window makes a pause in hand motion shorter than 1 s not end the event.
- **A30** After a put-back the mount tilt (pitch and roll) is the inverse-variance fusion, per axis, of the gyro-propagated tilt and the tilt measured from gravity over 2 s; yaw stays gyro-only. Gyro tilt uncertainty: 0.2° + 0.02°/s × (handling duration), not sourced, chosen from the T3.2-CR1 and T8.1 one-off runs (gyro-only pitch error 0.08° after a 4 s event and 4.3° after a 120 s event, which is about 1.7 sigma with these values, so the slope may be slightly optimistic). Gravity tilt uncertainty: the vehicle's unknown acceleration in the window divided by g, 0.2 m/s² without current GNSS (the true window mean was -0.18 and -0.06 m/s² in the two handling scenarios) and 0.05 m/s² with current GNSS, where the GNSS dv/dt is subtracted (not exercised by any current scenario: both put-backs happen inside the tunnel). Effect: a short event keeps the gyro tilt (the gravity weight is about 5%), a long one is pulled toward gravity (about 80%); the combined sigma is exposed as `sigma_tilt` so the EKF can inflate its accelerometer-bias covariance by (g · sigma_tilt)² when `mount_changed` is set. T9.3: at put-back both c1 = 0.02 and 0.03 put the tilt-induced bias sigma (g x tilt) above `accel_trust_sigma_b` (0.05 m/s^2; 0.052 and 0.060 m/s^2 after the 5 s event of `phone_handled_mid_tunnel`, 0.45 and 0.66 m/s^2 after the 2 minute event of `phone_in_hand_2min`), so the EKF holds constant speed either way; c1 only changes the result below about 0.018 deg/s.
- **A31** EKF parameters (docs/design/T8.2_ekf.md), not sourced. `bias_rw` 5e-4 m/s²/√s: the residual acceleration offset drifts about 0.009 m/s² over 300 s, consistent with the flagship accelerometer drift derived from A14. `v_rw` 0.3 m/s/√s: the driver's unknown acceleration when the accelerometer is unusable (A27 gives about 0.14 m/s² with 10-50 s periods). `init_bias_sigma` 0.1 m/s². `sigma_v` 0.2 m/s is used only when a fix carries no speed accuracy. `lateral_k` 3 and `gate_chi2` 13.8 (chi-square, 2 dof, 99.9%) reject broken fixes. `speed_limit_factor` 1.3 and `v_prior_sigma` 2 m/s make the speed-limit prior weak. `accel_trust_sigma_b` 0.05 m/s²: above this uncertainty of the acceleration offset (e.g. after a long hand event, where the tilt is uncertain by about 0.45 m/s²) and without a current fix, the filter assumes constant speed instead of integrating acceleration; 0.05 m/s² is where the offset would move a tunnel exit by about 100 m after 60 s. Four constants the note states in prose are parameters too: `initial_sigma_s` 50 m and `initial_sigma_v` 5 m/s before the first fix, `gnss_timeout_s` 1.5 s and `recover_fixes` 3. The white acceleration noise is the aligner's `sigma_base` only (A28); the alignment's yaw error, which leaks into `a_long` as an offset that is constant through a curve, is a fourth state `d_psi`, a consider state (initial variance `sigma_psi²` from the aligner, no process noise, mean never updated; see the design note addendum). Effect: the tilt-induced offset (pitch) is still only covered by `b` and its random walk. T8.6: `gate_reset_n` 5: after 5 consecutive on-road fixes rejected by the chi-square gate (5 s at 1 Hz, so a broken fix burst is not trusted) `s` and `v` are re-initialised from the fix. `display_k` 0.5 1/s: the display closes half of its gap to `s` per second (a forward jump is absorbed in a few seconds). `display_max_catchup` 10 m/s: the display never runs faster than `v` + 10 m/s (36 km/h), so a jump is not a visible leap.
- **A32** Stationary detection (T8.3), from the raw phone IMU: the standard deviation over a 1.0 s window of the accelerometer magnitude is below `zupt_acc_std` = 0.05 m/s² and that of the gyro magnitude below `zupt_gyro_std` = 0.004 rad/s, continuously for 0.5 s; it leaves as soon as either is exceeded. Reasoning: A17 gives about 0.03 m/s² and 0.002 rad/s at idle and 0.03 + 0.01·v m/s² and 0.002 + 0.0005·v rad/s per axis when moving, so the accelerometer limit is crossed at about 2 m/s; hand motion keeps both high. Not sourced; tuned on simulated data only. Measured on the 13 scenarios (share of time the detector says stationary while the true speed is above 0.5 m/s): 0.000% in the ten scenarios without a stop; `curved_3km_exit_inside` 0.068%, `hard_brake_inside` 0.235%, `stop_and_go_3km` 0.594%, always at true speeds up to 1.9, 2.8 and 2.8 m/s: the vehicle pulling away from a stop, because the magnitude of the acceleration changes only quadratically with horizontal acceleration (a²/2g, about 0.1 m/s² at 1.5 m/s²), so the detector lags the departure by about 2 s. Every true stop longer than 2 s was detected (1/1, 1/1, 6/6). Very slow crawling may be taken as a stop. The EKF therefore gates the v = 0 pseudo-measurement (`zupt_gate_chi2` = 10.8, chi-square 1 dof, 99.9%, on the EKF's own speed and uncertainty; 6, 10 and 51 updates rejected in those three scenarios) and skips a bias window in which the same test fails (the mean of a_long would hold the departure's real acceleration; without this the stop-and-go exit error was -146 m instead of -92 m). `zupt_v_sigma` = 0.02 m/s. The bias measurement (mean aligned a_long while stationary, once per second, sigma `sigma_base`/sqrt(n)) is deliberately weak: `sigma_base` (A28) is much larger than the idle noise, so it moves `b` by under 2% per update; with the idle noise as sigma it destabilised `curved_3km_exit_inside` (exit +712 m). Effect: ZUPT removes the speed drift during a stop (`hard_brake_inside` exit error +119 m to +9 m, consistency 33% to 94%), but position error accumulated while moving between stops is not corrected, and the filter becomes overconfident on `stop_and_go_3km` (consistency 100% to 14%) and `curved_3km_exit_inside` (100% to 56%). T9.3, the ZUPT updates: the `v = 0` update does not move s (its s gain is 0, like d_psi's; the Joseph form keeps P consistent). Reason: the detector can fire while the car still creeps at 0.4-0.8 m/s, and P_sv/P_vv of about 22 s turned that false `v = 0` into metres of position error (`stop_and_go_3km`: -80 -> -16 m over seeds 0-5 with the bias changes). Cost: on `hard_brake_inside` seed 0 the exit error is +29.5 m instead of +10 m, because that update used to correct a real position error; the 6-seed mean consistency is 80% (71% with the s gain kept). The bias update (the mean of `a_long` in each 1 s window while stationary) uses `R = max(s_win, 0.02)^2 / n + zupt_bias_floor_mps2^2`: `s_win` is the sample std of `a_long` in the window, 0.02 m/s^2 is a lower bound against a suspiciously quiet window, and `zupt_bias_floor_mps2` = 0.003 m/s^2 covers correlated vibration (the 1/n is optimistic) and a standing-versus-cruising difference of the offset (0.004 m/s^2 seen in one seed), so repeated stops cannot make b overconfident. A 1-dof chi-square gate (10.8) rejects a window whose mean the bias cannot explain, e.g. one that holds the start of a departure (+0.025 against -0.006 in seed 0); rejections are counted in `bias_gated`.
- **A33** Beacon update (T8.5), not sourced beyond the T3.6 check. A pass of one beacon is found in its RSSI packets: 1 s moving average; a pass is reported when the average has a maximum above `beacon_min_dbm` = -85 dBm (5 dB above the -90 dBm detection limit, A25, so a distant beacon is not used) and has dropped `beacon_drop_db` = 3 dB below it, or the beacon was not heard for 1 s. `t_peak` is the time of the maximum; if the average stays within 1 dB of the maximum for more than 3 s (the car stopped next to the beacon) the middle of that plateau is used. The update is `z = s_b + v * (t_det - t_peak)` with `sigma = sqrt(beacon_sigma^2 + (age * sigma_v)^2)`, `beacon_sigma` = 5 m: the T3.6 check showed a peak-time error of at most 0.24 s, about 5 m at 70 km/h (the moving average adds a small delay that this does not include). The update uses the normal 1-dof chi-square gate (10.8, 99.9%) and does not count towards the GNSS gate-reset streak (A31/T8.6). The beacon position is map knowledge (A2). RSSI noise (4 dB, A24) makes the maximum of the average arrive slightly early or late; that is what `beacon_sigma` covers. T9.3: the group delay of the causal 1 s moving average (0.5 s) is now subtracted from `t_peak` (it was measured as +0.50 s, std 0.51, about -9.7 m of implied position at 19.5 m/s in `beacons_5km`, T9.2), so the sentence above that the moving average adds a small delay no longer applies. `beacon_sigma` is 10 m: the per-pass spread left after the compensation was measured as 9.9 m in T9.2 (before it; it should be re-measured).
- **A34** Particle filter parameters (T8.4, docs/design/T8.4_pf.md), not sourced. CR1: the speed spread of the particles grows with the EKF's P_vv (`dv_i += N(0, sqrt(max(0, ΔP_vv)))` per 0.1 s step). CR2: no barometer matching: its benefit in tunnels is uncertain (ventilation and vehicle-induced pressure changes are unknown and may be far above the 8 Pa of A22), so the filter uses curvature only. `n` 500 particles: enough for a 1-D state with a speed offset, cheap in NumPy. `dv_rw` 0.05 m/s/√s: the speed offset of a particle drifts about 0.5 m/s over 100 s, a fraction of the EKF's speed uncertainty. `sigma_yaw` 0.005 rad/s: gyro noise after a 1 s average plus residual bias (A17, A28). `jitter_s` 1 m and `jitter_v` 0.05 m/s after resampling, so particles do not collapse onto copies. `seed` 0: fixed so runs are reproducible. Map matching runs only while the wrapped EKF has no current GNSS fix; an accepted GNSS fix or EKF beacon update re-draws the particles from the EKF's belief. CR3: `informative_ratio` 0.5: when the std of the particles' expected yaw rates is below half of `sigma_yaw` the curvature cannot tell them apart, so weighting and resampling are skipped. It is not sourced. A recentring of the cloud on the EKF (CR3) was removed in CR4: it discarded the corrections the curves had made.
