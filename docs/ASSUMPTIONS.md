# Assumptions

Numbered assumptions. Code that depends on one references it with a comment (`# A7`). New assumptions get the next number; never renumber.

- **A1** Tunnel is single-lane, no overtaking; the vehicle's route is known from navigation.
- **A2** Map centerline, curvature and grade are accurate (error < 2 m).
- **A3** Phone is fixed in a mount; taking it out of the mount is modeled as a separate scenario.
- **A4** There are at least 60 s of good GNSS before the tunnel (calibration window).
- **A5** Sensors: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, barometer, GNSS. No vehicle speed data (OBD, CarPlay); discussed as "what if" in the write-up.
- **A6** No GNSS, cellular or Wi-Fi positioning inside the tunnel.
- **A7** Beacons exist only in scenarios that explicitly enable them.
- **A8** In simulation, sensor timestamps are synchronized and delay-free.
- **A9** The callout point is where the exit on the route begins; the driver does not miss the exit.
- **A10** With several vehicles, the single lane means a follower cannot get closer than a minimum gap to the vehicle ahead.
- **A11** Longitudinal motion is jerk-limited at 2.0 m/s³. Speed changes the scenario gives no acceleration for (cruise to a new speed, `stop_and_go`) use 1.5 m/s². Both are typical passenger-comfort bounds (e.g. Hoberock 1977, "A survey of longitudinal acceleration comfort studies in ground transportation vehicles").
- **A12** Pitch is blended over 40 m (`VERTICAL_BLEND_M`) at grade changes, like road vertical curves, so the vertical acceleration `v² · d(pitch)/ds` has no spikes at grade steps.
- **A13** Follower braking up to 3.0 m/s², below the AASHTO comfortable deceleration of 3.4 m/s².
- **A14** The phone's temperature changes by about 2 K during a 3-minute tunnel. Bias drift values in presets are derived from datasheet temperature coefficients with this assumption.
- **A15** GNSS position error is time-correlated (Gauss–Markov, 30 s); GNSS speed noise 0.15 m/s and magnetometer noise 0.3 µT in open sky are typical smartphone values, not datasheet figures.
- **A16** The budget preset is the flagship preset scaled by fixed multipliers (2–4×), since low-cost phone sensor datasheets are rarely public.
- **A17** Vibration and handling. Engine idle: 0.03 m/s² accel and 0.002 rad/s gyro RMS. Moving: accel RMS 0.03 + 0.01·v m/s², gyro RMS 0.002 + 0.0005·v rad/s (v in m/s). Tunable; chosen so that stopped and moving are separable for ZUPT. Phone handling is an orientation path, not noise: a 1 s pick-up (or T/4 for events shorter than 4 s) to a hand orientation about 15° per axis from the mount; while held, a re-orientation every 10–30 s by about 20° per axis (only when the hold exceeds 10 s); hand wobble of about 5° per axis (three sinusoids per axis at 0.5–2 Hz, faded in and out so the path starts at the old mount and ends at the new one); a 1 s put-back to the new mount. The gyro sees this path exactly (its relative rotation rate is added to the vehicle's), and the accelerometer is rotated with it. On top, 2 m/s² white translational acceleration noise on the accelerometer for the event duration (not sourced). Real hand displacement is bounded, white acceleration noise is not, so estimators must not integrate acceleration while the phone is in hand. An event may run past the end of the run (no put-back). Effect: only the handling scenarios.
- **A18** GNSS vertical error is 1.5 × horizontal: satellites are only above the horizon, so vertical geometry is weaker; FAA GPS SPS Performance Analysis reports show 95% vertical/horizontal ratios of about 1.35–1.6.
- **A19** GNSS error and reported h_acc grow linearly to 5× within portal_degrade_m of a portal, on both sides, from sky blockage and multipath; tunable.
- **A20** Barometer base altitude is sea level (0 m); Istanbul's undersea tunnels start near sea level. Effect: every 100 m of base altitude changes the pressure-altitude gradient by about 1%; estimators use relative pressure only, so results are insensitive.
- **A21** The tunnel pressure step settles exponentially with a 1 s time constant at entry and reverses the same way at exit (cabin leakage and the barometer's internal IIR low-pass filter; the exact value is not sourced). Effect: only the first few seconds after each portal; the barometer reference in T8.4 must be taken at least 5 s after entry.
- **A22** Fan-driven pressure fluctuation is first-order Gauss-Markov with a 10 s correlation time (not sourced). Effect: a longer correlation time makes fluctuations look like altitude changes and hurts barometer map matching; a candidate for the Phase 9 sweeps.
- **A23** Inside a tunnel the magnetic disturbance from steel reinforcement and passing vehicles is modeled as a spatial Gauss-Markov field with a 10 m correlation length and mag_disturbance_ut std per axis, generated per vehicle (not sourced); a shared per-tunnel field would be more realistic, this is a deliberate simplification. Effect: none on estimators, which do not use the magnetometer; it only supports the demonstration of why the magnetometer is excluded.
- **A24** BLE RSSI noise is independent Gaussian per packet with 4 dB std (published measurements range from a few dB to over 10 dB depending on environment; not sourced for tunnels). Effect: larger noise widens the RSSI peak and delays or shifts beacon detection in T8.5.
- **A25** The phone receives every advertisement at or above -90 dBm and none below (continuous scanning, as in Android's low-latency scan mode; iOS ranging reports about once per second instead). Effect: sets the detection range, about 25 m with the default scenario values.
- **A26** Beacons are wall-mounted 2.5 m laterally from the lane center and 2 m above the phone, so the closest approach is about 3.2 m. Effect: caps the peak RSSI and flattens the peak slightly.
- **A27** Driver speed variation while cruising is a sum of 4 cosines with random phases and frequencies drawn uniformly from 0.02-0.1 Hz (10-50 s periods), scaled so its std is speed_jitter_kmh; it fades in and out over 3 s around stops and speed changes (not sourced; this is not an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process). Effect: it dominates the baseline's dead-reckoning error in scenarios without stops (roughly 0.5 m/s times the outage duration).
- **A28** The longitudinal acceleration noise the aligner reports before any yaw uncertainty is `sigma_base` = 0.3 m/s² (not sourced). It matches the A17 vibration level at highway speed (0.03 + 0.01·v, about 0.23 m/s² per axis at 20 m/s) plus a margin; measured on the 13 scenarios the error of the extracted longitudinal acceleration is 0.14–0.26 m/s² RMS. Effect: sets how much the EKF trusts the accelerometer; the curve-dependent yaw term is added on top.
- **A29** Handling detection thresholds, from the gyro only: start when the smoothed (0.2 s moving average) angular rate, minus bias and the expected vehicle rotation, exceeds 0.15 rad/s for more than 0.2 s; end when it stays below 0.03 rad/s for 1.0 s. Reasoning: a hand pick-up or put-back of about 15° in 1 s peaks at about 0.4 rad/s and the hand wobble at 0.5-2 Hz is of similar size, while the vehicle's own rotation is about 0.02 rad/s (v/R at 20 m/s and 800 m) and gyro vibration is about 0.01 rad/s per axis (A17); the moving average is needed because raw vibration noise (3-axis magnitude about 0.02 rad/s) is only about 1.3 sigma below the end threshold. A slow, deliberate hand movement below 0.15 rad/s would go undetected. Tunable; the values were tuned on simulated data only. Effect: detection delay of about 0.1 s at the start and 1 s at the end of an event; the 1.0 s calm window makes a pause in hand motion shorter than 1 s not end the event.
- **A30** After a put-back the mount tilt (pitch and roll) is the inverse-variance fusion, per axis, of the gyro-propagated tilt and the tilt measured from gravity over 2 s; yaw stays gyro-only. Gyro tilt uncertainty: 0.2° + 0.02°/s × (handling duration), not sourced, chosen from the T3.2-CR1 and T8.1 one-off runs (gyro-only pitch error 0.08° after a 4 s event and 4.3° after a 120 s event, which is about 1.7 sigma with these values, so the slope may be slightly optimistic). Gravity tilt uncertainty: the vehicle's unknown acceleration in the window divided by g, 0.2 m/s² without current GNSS (the true window mean was -0.18 and -0.06 m/s² in the two handling scenarios) and 0.05 m/s² with current GNSS, where the GNSS dv/dt is subtracted (not exercised by any current scenario: both put-backs happen inside the tunnel). Effect: a short event keeps the gyro tilt (the gravity weight is about 5%), a long one is pulled toward gravity (about 80%); the combined sigma is exposed as `sigma_tilt` so the EKF can inflate its accelerometer-bias covariance by (g · sigma_tilt)² when `mount_changed` is set.
- **A31** EKF parameters (docs/design/T8.2_ekf.md), not sourced. `bias_rw` 5e-4 m/s²/√s: the residual acceleration offset drifts about 0.009 m/s² over 300 s, consistent with the flagship accelerometer drift derived from A14. `v_rw` 0.3 m/s/√s: the driver's unknown acceleration when the accelerometer is unusable (A27 gives about 0.14 m/s² with 10-50 s periods). `init_bias_sigma` 0.1 m/s². `sigma_v` 0.2 m/s is used only when a fix carries no speed accuracy. `lateral_k` 3 and `gate_chi2` 13.8 (chi-square, 2 dof, 99.9%) reject broken fixes. `speed_limit_factor` 1.3 and `v_prior_sigma` 2 m/s make the speed-limit prior weak. `accel_trust_sigma_b` 0.05 m/s²: above this uncertainty of the acceleration offset (e.g. after a long hand event, where the tilt is uncertain by about 0.45 m/s²) and without a current fix, the filter assumes constant speed instead of integrating acceleration; 0.05 m/s² is where the offset would move a tunnel exit by about 100 m after 60 s. Four constants the note states in prose are parameters too: `initial_sigma_s` 50 m and `initial_sigma_v` 5 m/s before the first fix, `gnss_timeout_s` 1.5 s and `recover_fixes` 3. The white acceleration noise is the aligner's `sigma_base` only (A28); the alignment's yaw error, which leaks into `a_long` as an offset that is constant through a curve, is a fourth state `d_psi`, a consider state (initial variance `sigma_psi²` from the aligner, no process noise, mean never updated; see the design note addendum). Effect: the tilt-induced offset (pitch) is still only covered by `b` and its random walk.
- **A32** Stationary detection (T8.3), from the raw phone IMU: the standard deviation over a 1.0 s window of the accelerometer magnitude is below `zupt_acc_std` = 0.05 m/s² and that of the gyro magnitude below `zupt_gyro_std` = 0.004 rad/s, continuously for 0.5 s; it leaves as soon as either is exceeded. Reasoning: A17 gives about 0.03 m/s² and 0.002 rad/s at idle and 0.03 + 0.01·v m/s² and 0.002 + 0.0005·v rad/s per axis when moving, so the accelerometer limit is crossed at about 2 m/s; hand motion keeps both high. Not sourced; tuned on simulated data only. Measured on the 13 scenarios (share of time the detector says stationary while the true speed is above 0.5 m/s): 0.000% in the ten scenarios without a stop; `curved_3km_exit_inside` 0.068%, `hard_brake_inside` 0.235%, `stop_and_go_3km` 0.594%, always at true speeds up to 1.9, 2.8 and 2.8 m/s: the vehicle pulling away from a stop, because the magnitude of the acceleration changes only quadratically with horizontal acceleration (a²/2g, about 0.1 m/s² at 1.5 m/s²), so the detector lags the departure by about 2 s. Every true stop longer than 2 s was detected (1/1, 1/1, 6/6). Very slow crawling may be taken as a stop. The EKF therefore gates the v = 0 pseudo-measurement (`zupt_gate_chi2` = 10.8, chi-square 1 dof, 99.9%, on the EKF's own speed and uncertainty; 6, 10 and 51 updates rejected in those three scenarios) and skips a bias window in which the same test fails (the mean of a_long would hold the departure's real acceleration; without this the stop-and-go exit error was -146 m instead of -92 m). `zupt_v_sigma` = 0.02 m/s. The bias measurement (mean aligned a_long while stationary, once per second, sigma `sigma_base`/sqrt(n)) is deliberately weak: `sigma_base` (A28) is much larger than the idle noise, so it moves `b` by under 2% per update; with the idle noise as sigma it destabilised `curved_3km_exit_inside` (exit +712 m). Effect: ZUPT removes the speed drift during a stop (`hard_brake_inside` exit error +119 m to +9 m, consistency 33% to 94%), but position error accumulated while moving between stops is not corrected, and the filter becomes overconfident on `stop_and_go_3km` (consistency 100% to 14%) and `curved_3km_exit_inside` (100% to 56%).
